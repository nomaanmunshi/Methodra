# Evidence model

Methodra separates an interesting idea from the strength of evidence for a specific behavior-change claim.

## Levels

**Level A — Strong support**  
Relevant systematic review/meta-analysis or comparably strong evidence. A still does not mean universal effectiveness.

**Level B — Promising support**  
Controlled, field, replicated or otherwise useful evidence with meaningful context dependence or uncertainty.

**Level C — Practical framework**  
A respected author/professional framework that may be useful but is not presented as a validated complete system.

**Level D — Reflection**  
Philosophical/inspirational material without a behavior-change effectiveness claim.

The level, rationale and plain-language limitation are content data. UI code does not hardcode every method as “proven” or “science-backed”.

## V1 research catalog

The ten-method catalog starts from implementation intentions, mental contrasting/implementation intentions, habit automaticity, progress monitoring, temptation bundling, fresh-start effects, attention residue, self-determination theory, retrieval practice, and voluntary commitment-device research.

Source links are stored alongside method content and rendered in the Methods screen. The repository's starting references are listed in `docs/RESEARCH_REFERENCES.md`.

## Personal Lab language

A single person's tracked trial does not establish causality. Methodra uses phrases such as “during this trial”, “associated with”, and “insufficient data”. It does not tell a user that a tracked method caused an improvement.

## Health boundary

General productivity research must not be silently converted into claims about diagnosing, treating or curing ADHD, anxiety, depression, addiction, sleep disorders, or another medical condition.
